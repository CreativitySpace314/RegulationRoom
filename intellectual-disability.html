🧩 INTELLECTUAL DISABILITY · RABBIT HOLE

Intellectual Disability

Intellectual disability involves limitations in intellectual functioning and adaptive functioning. Support should be individualized around what the person understands, communicates and does in everyday life while protecting adulthood, autonomy and dignity.

Support participation. Don't take over the person.

Three places to look

Thinking + learning

Learning new information, reasoning, problem-solving, memory and understanding complex or abstract information may require different pacing or teaching.

Communication

Receptive language and expressive language are not always equal. A person may understand more than they can easily say, or may say yes without fully understanding.

Adaptive functioning

Everyday conceptual, social and practical skills matter: communication, money, safety, routines, self-care, decision-making and community participation.

Rabbit holes 🐇

Processing time

Why silence after a question may mean “still processing,” not refusal.

Prompting

How to help without creating prompt dependence or doing the task for the person.

Supported choice

Making choices understandable and real rather than deciding for someone.

Receptive vs expressive language

Why fluent speech does not automatically mean complex instructions were understood.

Adaptive skills

Looking at what a person needs in real life, not reducing ability to an IQ score.

Dual diagnosis

Mental illness can occur alongside intellectual disability. New symptoms should not automatically be attributed to the disability.

Baseline matters here too.

Baseline means what is usual for that individual. Their typical communication, energy, sleep, behavior, daily skills and level of support are the comparison point. A meaningful change from that usual pattern deserves curiosity instead of “that's just their disability.”
