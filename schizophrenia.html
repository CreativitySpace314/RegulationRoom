🧠 PSYCHOSIS · PERCEPTION · THINKING · DIG DEEPER

Schizophrenia + Psychosis

Psychosis can change perception, thinking and the sense of what is real. Schizophrenia can also affect motivation, expression, attention, memory and everyday functioning. The goal is not to out-argue an experience. It is to understand what the person is experiencing, notice what changed, lower unnecessary pressure and support connection, safety and treatment.

Their experience is real to them. Your job is not to make the fear bigger.

One person. Several systems can be affected.

Psychosis is not one switch flipping on or off. Perception, meaning, thinking and everyday functioning can each look different and can change over time.

First: these words are not interchangeable.

A person can experience psychosis without having schizophrenia, and people with schizophrenia can have very different symptom profiles.

Psychosis

Symptoms involving disrupted thoughts or perceptions and difficulty determining what is real.

Schizophrenia

Can include psychotic symptoms plus negative and cognitive symptoms.

Schizoaffective disorder

Includes psychotic symptoms and significant mood symptoms such as depression or mania.

Paranoia

Suspiciousness or beliefs involving threat, harm or persecution can occur during psychosis.

psychotic symptoms

hallucinations · delusions · disorganized thinking/speech

negative symptoms

reduced motivation · withdrawal · reduced expression · difficulty initiating

cognitive symptoms

attention · concentration · working memory · processing information

About “paranoid schizophrenia”: you may still hear it in older records or everyday language, but DSM-5 removed the old schizophrenia subtypes.

Hallucinations are not one thing.

A hallucination is a sensory experience without an external stimulus others can identify. It can involve more than hearing voices, and the experience can be neutral, comforting, confusing, frightening or unsafe.

Which sense is involved?

👂AUDITORY

It may sound like...

A person may hear voices, sounds, music or other auditory experiences that others do not hear. Voices may be neutral, comforting, critical, frightening or commanding.

What staff might notice

Pausing as if listening, talking back to a voice, distraction, fear, laughing, covering ears or asking others if they heard something.

Ask about the experience

“What are you hearing?” “Is it bothering you?” “Is the voice telling you to do anything?”

Staff lens

Do not assume every voice is dangerous. Stay calm, understand the experience, and follow the person’s individualized plan and safety process if risk increases.

Keep the whole picture in view. 🧠

Use these as quick reference points after the interactive pieces above, not as another quiz.

👂 Hallucinations

Ask what the experience is like and whether it is distressing or affecting safety.

🔎 Delusions + paranoia

Stay calm, transparent and grounded in what you observe.

🧵 Disorganized thinking

One question at a time, fewer choices, extra processing time.

🌫️ Negative symptoms

Low motivation and reduced expression can be mistaken for laziness or defiance.

🧠 Cognitive symptoms

Attention, memory and processing difficulties can affect everyday tasks.

💊 Medication + side effects

Notice and document concerns and follow approved clinical processes.

🌱 Recovery

A diagnosis does not erase goals, relationships, autonomy or identity.

⚠️ Change from baseline

Know the person's usual pattern and notice clusters of meaningful change.

Keep this in your pocket: “I can believe the fear is real without saying the belief is fact.”
