🧠 SCHIZOPHRENIA SPECTRUM · EXPLORE, NOTICE, RESPOND

Schizophrenia Spectrum

Schizophrenia is more than psychosis. Perception, meaning, thinking, motivation, expression, sleep, medication experiences and daily functioning can all shift. This page is built to explore one piece at a time instead of turning a person into a checklist.

A diagnosis is context, not identity. Notice the person first.
Related ≠ the same thing.
Schizophrenia↘PSYCHOSIS↗Schizoaffective disorder
Psychosis can occur in schizophrenia and schizoaffective disorder, and it can also occur in other clinical situations. Psychosis is a symptom experience, not another word for schizophrenia.

OPTIONAL · EMPATHY EXPERIENCE

Empathy > Sympathy. Put on your ears. Listen to understand, not respond.

You are about to move through a very ordinary morning while recorded whisper layers continue underneath it. Some moments will be quieter. Some will press closer. Your job is not to solve the voices. Your job is to keep doing everyday things while noticing what that costs.

Before you start: This is a dramatized educational exercise, not a recreation of every person’s experience and not a diagnostic tool. Auditory hallucinations vary widely and may be neutral, comforting, critical, frightening, commanding or something else entirely. Skip this activity if simulated voices may be distressing or personally destabilizing. Keep the volume comfortable and stop anytime.

AFTER THE NOISE

Nothing you just did was complicated.

Tap what you noticed.
This is the empathy piece. The clothes, breakfast, medication reminder, grocery list, phone and door were ordinary. The extra work was filtering, deciding what deserved attention, remembering what you were doing, managing emotion and continuing anyway.

So when someone seems distracted, slow, suspicious, irritated, stuck, or unable to start: curiosity is more useful than judgment. Fewer words, one step, time, transparency and a regulated support person may reduce what their brain has to carry.

This exercise is intentionally limited. It cannot tell you what psychosis “feels like,” and completing it easily does not mean a real person should be able to “just ignore” hallucinations.
