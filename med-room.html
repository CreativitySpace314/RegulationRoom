📝 Administration notes

You do not need a narrative for every routine Taken dose. Use the note when the result is not routine or important context needs captured.

Why it matters

One person. One pass.

Preparing and administering for one person at a time reduces mix-ups and keeps the process focused.

The eMAR stays part of the pass.

Keep it visible while matching the person, medication, order and instructions instead of preparing from memory.

Chart what actually happened.

Routine Taken doses are documented promptly. Exceptions need the correct result and useful context.

Stop when something does not match.

A discrepancy is a stop sign—not a “close enough.” Clarify before administering.

Wrong / missed / not available

Wrong med, dose, time, route—or not given?

Treat it as a medication error and follow the agency reporting process immediately.

No supply?

Use the appropriate NotAvailable result and explain the reason in the administration note.

Refusal?

Use the agency refusal process and document the exception/follow-up where required.

Controlled medication discrepancy?

Stop and report the count discrepancy to the supervisor / On Call Manager.
