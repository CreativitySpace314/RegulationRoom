⚠️ ➕ SUICIDE PREVENTION · INTERACTIVE PRACTICE

Stay human. Stay with the person.

Practice the moments around suicidal thoughts: noticing what someone may be telling you, asking clearly, listening without turning the conversation into an interrogation, working toward safety-for-now, and recognizing when another level of help is needed.

SAFETY ACTION ≠ RELATIONSHIP LOST.
1. NOTICEWords, actions, feelings, physical or behavioral changes.
2. ASK CLEARLYDo not make the person hint harder. Ask directly about suicide.
3. LISTENHear the story, what changed, what hurts, and what still connects them to life.
4. BUILD SAFETYMake the next steps specific: what, when, how and who.
5. CONNECTAdd the right support while keeping the person involved wherever you can.

WHAT MIGHT MAKE YOU NOTICE?

See: withdrawal, major behavior change, risk-taking, self-harm, giving things away or a change from baseline.

Hear: hopelessness, feeling like a burden, “I can’t do this,” “I don’t belong,” or direct talk about death or suicide.

WHAT ARE YOU LISTENING FOR?

The person’s story, why this moment feels different, uncertainty or ambivalence, reasons to keep going, people or places that matter, and whether they can participate in staying safe right now.

CHOOSE WHERE TO START

Pick a situation, then work with what the person actually gives you. In some paths you will need to notice invitations before you even know suicide is part of the conversation.

What matters here

SAFETY-FOR-NOW LENS · MORE SUPPORT IS NEEDED

PREVENT

Is harm happening now or likely very soon? Can the person participate in safe decisions? If immediate safety cannot be maintained, activate the emergency response for the setting.

PROTECT

What could increase danger right now? Consider current suicide planning, access to identified risks, substance or medication concerns, prior suicidal behavior, mental-health symptoms and the person’s existing plan.

PROMOTE

What reduces stress or adds life back into the next few hours? People, places, coping strengths, routines, supportive environments and reasons to stay connected all matter.

PREPARE

Who can help if things get harder? Make crisis, clinical, personal and emergency contacts concrete and know how the person will reach them.

Right now, what will help this person be safer — and who needs to be part of that?

This is practice, not a script. Real people and real settings are different. Use what you know about the person, their support or safety plan, and the procedures for your setting.

Keep the person involved wherever you can.
Connection still matters when the level of support has to increase.
